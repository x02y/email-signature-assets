    WHAT THE PACK IS DOING

    The tablet runs from a 48 Wh pack. Nothing is plugged into the wall. A USB-C PD source at 15 V / 3 A offers 45 W. The Pi and the backlight draw about 8 W from that bus. The rest, after a little conversion loss, fast-charges the cells. ELAPSE jumps the pack clock so a half-hour charge is visible. The watt figures themselves are the real budget. Live drain ticks in real time on the watt-hours.

    SENSE counts storeys from the heights of heartbeats in range. DRAW adds rooms on the plan. PLANS keeps them on this device.

      THREE MODES

      SENSE ignores any drawing and counts storeys from heartbeat height. The lowest return and the highest return, with you in between, and a storey pitch taken from the building. Empty storeys between two heartbeats still count.

      DRAW: drag a rectangle on the plan. It is a room on the floor you are standing on. SAVE puts the plan in the bank on this device. UNDO ROOM removes the last one.

      IMPORT takes a picture of a blueprint, or a pulsegrid-plans.json file. The picture sits under the floor you were on when you imported it. EXPORT downloads that file. TO USB asks for a folder, which on the Pi can be a memory stick you just plugged in, and writes pulsegrid-plans.json there.

      PULSEGRID MK1 is the shell in your hands: a Raspberry Pi 5, a 7-inch capacitive glass, and a 48 Wh pack, running this screen.

      COMPUTE

      Raspberry Pi 5. This page is the kiosk. Open it full screen in Chromium and the glass is the instrument.

      POWER

      USB-C port on the chin. Unplugged, the pack runs the Pi and the display for several hours. To charge, use a USB-C Power Delivery charger. The simulated contract is 15 V at 3 A, 45 W. Do not feed 15 V straight into a Raspberry Pi 5. The Pi wants 5 V, and a Pi 5 is happiest with a supply that can do 5 V at 5 A. In a real shell the 15 V contract stops at a charger board, which bucks down to 5 V for the Pi and tops up the cells with what is left.

      THE SENSOR PICTURE

      Heartbeats, walls, and floors are a room simulation. Slant range is the straight-line distance from the tablet to the person, including the height between floors, shown to 0.1 m. Speed is how far that person actually moved over the last couple of seconds, worked out as distance over time. THRU WALL means the line crosses a wall on your floor. THRU FLOOR means they are on another deck. The scope does not listen through real walls.

      HOW TO READ IT

      ▲ above you, cyan. ● your level, green. ▼ below you, amber. The yellow arrow is you, and it points the way the arc is facing. Drag the plan to walk. N E S W step you 0.6 m. The stack on the right of the scope is the building: tap a plate to scan that floor, tap the letter to go and stand there. Scan more than one floor to keep the people above and below. AUDIO plays a heartbeat each time the sweep detects someone in range.

      BATTERY EMPTY

      The pack is flat and the Pi is off. Connect USB-C and it will boot from the charger.
